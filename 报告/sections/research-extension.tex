\section{Hypothesis-Driven Research Extension}\label{sec:extension}
\subsection{From Replication to a Discriminating Question}
The localization experiments above reproduce established TFEB biology. The extension asks a different question: \textit{does the effect of the C-terminal S466/S467/S469 cluster on target-gene RNA abundance depend on the stimulus and fusion architecture?} Protein abundance and nuclear exposure are measured as possible mediators, and the architecture effect is the hypothesis under test.

Paquette et al.\ reported that AMPK-dependent phosphorylation of S466/\allowbreak S467/\allowbreak S469 governs TFEB transcriptional activity, whereas mTORC1 governs cytosolic retention; a GFP-tagged S466A/\allowbreak S467A/\allowbreak S469A mutant (3A) translocated to the nucleus yet lost transcriptional activity \cite{Paquette2021,Negoita2026}. Negoita et al.\ subsequently found that AMPK activation dephosphorylates this cluster, that a synthetic peptide containing the sites is a poor AMPK substrate in vitro, and that AMPK loss abrogated the MK-8722 response but not the Torin1 response \cite{Negoita2026}. These findings come from different cell systems, stimuli and constructs, and the matrix below supplies a matched comparison. 3A is an existing mutant. S467 is also an Akt phosphorylation site that restrains TFEB nuclear entry independently of mTORC1 \cite{Palmieri2017}, so stimulus-dependent 3A effects can reflect Akt as well as AMPK; p-AKT(S473) is therefore measured under both drugs.

The proposed contribution is a controlled genotype--architecture--stimulus comparison in HeLa cells, followed by selected mechanistic tests. Negoita et al.\ also obtained supporting phosphosite evidence from C-terminal GFP knock-in material \cite{Negoita2026}, so GFP is treated as one of several candidate sources of the literature disagreement. The 3T substitution retains hydroxyl-bearing side chains and remains phosphorylatable, which makes it a sequence/chemistry probe; Negoita et al.\ used S466T/S467T and S469T substitutions as phospho-antibody specificity controls in HEK293 cells, and observed that S469A raised the p-S466/S467 signal \cite{Negoita2026}. A targeted literature search identified no matched comparison of these three factors.

The two literature models predict opposite outcomes for 3A. If AMPK-dependent phosphorylation activates TFEB, as Paquette et al.\ proposed, 3A loses target-RNA induction under both drugs. If dephosphorylation activates TFEB, as Negoita et al.\ proposed, a non-phosphorylatable 3A retains or exceeds WT induction. A fusion-architecture effect appears as a different sign or size of the 3A--WT difference between tagged and untagged TFEB. The primary contrasts below are two-sided, and these predictions guide their interpretation.

The two architectures differ in linker, EGFP coding sequence, protein length and the position of the stop junction, so ``tag'' in the matrix denotes the entire GDPPVAT--EGFP fusion architecture. Attributing an effect to EGFP itself is the purpose of module R06, which adds two linker-only constructs once an architecture effect is established. Drug-associated changes in transgene RNA or protein stability are checked before a protein-level tag mechanism is proposed.

\subsection{Core Factorial Comparison and Controls}
WT/3A/3T TFEB with EGFP-tagged/untagged architecture and vehicle/Torin1/MK-8722 treatment give 18 core conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:research-design}). The principal comparison is performed after endogenous TFEB depletion with one validated 3$'$-UTR siRNA that has no site in the coding-only constructs. The complete prespecified WT/3A comparison (both architectures and all three treatments) is repeated with a second individual TFEB siRNA in separate culture-day blocks. Transcript coverage across expressed TFEB isoforms and maintained transgene expression during RNAi are verified. Endogenous depletion is quantified by 3$'$-UTR RT-qPCR and by protein in matched no-rescue wells. Untagged rescue and residual endogenous TFEB co-migrate, and the no-rescue wells bound the residual endogenous level.

Acceptance gates are acceptable viability, at least 80\% endogenous protein depletion in no-rescue wells and a pretreatment median TFEB signal per expressing cell within 0.5--2 times the parental reference. Untagged WT must restore at least half of the pilot-estimated loss of drug-induced MCOLN1 response, in the same direction, in each drug context for which a TFEB-dependent interpretation is sought, and the loss must exceed the pilot assay noise. If this is not met for a drug, the comparison for that drug remains descriptive until the assay is repaired. If MK-8722 fails the gate, the extension proceeds with Torin1 alone, the primary estimands reduce to the architecture-dependent 3A effect under Torin1 (defined below), and the AMPK module is dropped. A tagged WT that rescues poorly is retained as an architecture result. The knockdown background (TFEB-only or TFEB + TFE3, chosen in the core pilot) and all gates are frozen before confirmatory genotype outcomes are unblinded.

The following controls are run at vehicle, Torin1 and MK-8722 in each independent block. A machine-readable condition manifest accompanies the proposal; counts include culture conditions, and qPCR technical reactions are counted separately.

\Needspace{3\baselineskip}
Pilot DNA amounts are set separately to overlap TFEB abundance and the fraction of expressing cells across constructs, using matched total DNA and transfection reagent. Both architectures are quantified with the same anti-TFEB reagent, recognizing an epitope outside the changed C-terminal region, with specificity validated by depletion and linearity by antigen-loading series. Each construct-specific DNA dose is frozen before drug exposure and used in all three treatment arms; retitration after treatment would erase a biological abundance response. In the pretreatment expression pilot, the target is a between-architecture ratio of 0.8--1.25 for the median TFEB signal among expressing cells and an expressing-fraction difference no greater than 10 percentage points. The measured differences and their uncertainty are reported. A common threshold above depleted/no-rescue background is frozen, fields are selected randomly, and all-cell distributions, stratified summaries and exclusions are reported. GFP brightness is not used to select one architecture. Bulk RNA from transiently transfected wells averages over transfected and untransfected cells, so the transfected fraction is measured for every construct. If common expression support is absent, delivery is revised before the confirmatory comparison, and the constructs move to single-copy isogenic lines (Flp-In-type integration) or low-MOI lentiviral pools, which deliver matched dose without transient-transfection variance. At baseline and 6 h, transgene RNA is assayed with validated backbone--TFEB-junction and shared TFEB-CDS assays, endogenous RNA with the 3$'$-UTR assay, and protein separately; tag-dependent RNA abundance is the alternative to altered protein activity.

For the extension, a common nutrient-replete medium and matched final solvent are used. MK-8722 is piloted at 1, 3 and 10~$\mu$M, and the Torin1 125/250/500-nM pilot range is retained. The lowest tolerated dose with reproducible intended engagement is selected; these doses are HeLa starting points fixed by the pilot. p-ACC/total ACC and p-S6K/total S6K are measured under both drugs, together with p-AKT(S473)/total AKT, p-TFEB(S211), TFEB abundance, cell counts and viability. Engagement and tolerability are rechecked at 6 h, since an acute pilot qualifies only the acute exposure. The two drugs are compared at their own pilot-selected doses, with dose and kinetic differences reported and a second acceptable dose as the sensitivity experiment. Torin1 also inhibits mTORC2/Akt signaling, so the drug comparison tests stimulus dependence, and pathway attribution comes from the AMPK and Akt modules below.

Localization is monitored at 0, 30, 60, 120, 240 and 360 min in matched destructive samples, and the primary RNA endpoint is collected at 6 h. N/C, nuclear concentration, integrated nuclear TFEB signal and cell/nuclear size are retained, because N/C can be equal when nuclear amounts differ. The sampled nuclear-signal trajectory and its trapezoidal area summarize population exposure; they are a population proxy, whereas single-cell trajectories and absolute molecule counts require live-cell imaging and calibration. An intrinsic transactivation interpretation requires overlapping nuclear-signal distributions and comparable trajectories, supported by orthogonal fractionation. Matching limits are prespecified from assay precision before confirmation. Exposure differences are reported as a mediator, and all samples stay in the primary analysis.

\subsection{Primary Estimand, Replication and RNA Interpretation}
The primary endpoint is normalized steady-state RNA abundance of the primary target at 6 h. MCOLN1 is the default; LAMP1 and CTSD are secondary. The pilot confirms MCOLN1 or replaces it once, with the target among MCOLN1, LAMP1 and CTSD that shows the largest TFEB-dependent drug induction in non-targeting, WT-rescue and KD-no-rescue wells, using no 3A or 3T data. The selection is frozen before confirmation, and if none of the three shows induction above pilot noise, R02 does not proceed. RNA integrity, genomic-DNA removal, primer efficiency, quantification limits and at least two reference genes are validated across all treatments, architectures and depletion backgrounds, following qPCR reporting principles \cite{Bustin2009}. No-template/no-RT controls and an inter-run calibrator are included. A missing amplification is recorded as missing, and values outside the validated quantitative range are flagged under a prespecified repeat/exclusion rule.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Let $Y_{g,a,s,d}$ denote the mean log$_2$ normalized RNA value from culture wells on independent day $d$, for genotype $g$, architecture $a$ (E, EGFP fusion; U, untagged) and stimulus $s$ (V, vehicle; M, MK-8722; T, Torin1). qPCR technical replicates are averaged within each sample first. Define
\begin{align}
\Delta_{a,s,d}&=(Y_{3A,a,s,d}-Y_{3A,a,V,d})-(Y_{WT,a,s,d}-Y_{WT,a,V,d}),\\
C_d&=(\Delta_{E,M,d}-\Delta_{U,M,d})-(\Delta_{E,T,d}-\Delta_{U,T,d}).
\end{align}
The architecture-dependent 3A effect at stimulus $s$ is
\begin{equation}
A_{s,d}=(Y_{3A,E,s,d}-Y_{WT,E,s,d})-(Y_{3A,U,s,d}-Y_{WT,U,s,d}),
\end{equation}
and $C_d=A_{M,d}-A_{T,d}$. The three primary estimands are the means of $A_{M,d}$, $A_{T,d}$ and $C_d$: whether the 3A effect differs between architectures under each drug, and whether that architecture dependence differs between the drugs. Holm correction applies across these three tests. $A_{s,d}$ uses stimulus-matched cell means only, and vehicle terms cancel algebraically from $C_d$ and remain essential for estimating each drug response and the baseline genotype effects. The component effects are reported alongside $C_d$, because equal nonzero architecture dependence under both drugs gives $C_d=0$. The primary result is the total effect in the calibrated expression system. Post-treatment nuclear exposure and protein abundance are mediators and are not entered as covariates in the primary model; matched-exposure analyses are secondary.

Complete independent-day blocks use separate cell culture, RNAi and transfection preparations, with technical wells distributed across plate positions. Day-level $C_d$ values, their mean and a two-sided 95\% interval based on between-day variation are reported (a $t$ interval with $n-1$ degrees of freedom when its assumptions hold). Biological $n$ is the independent day; wells, cells, clones and PCR reactions are averaged within day. A failed well is repeated, and cell-level data do not replace it. Repeat rules are prespecified from technical QC; if a required arm is unavailable, that incomplete day is excluded from the paired primary estimate, kept descriptively with the reason recorded, and a replacement complete block is acquired. Outcome size or direction is never an exclusion criterion.

A separate pilot of at least four complete day blocks estimates the variance of $C_d$, and the confirmatory sample size is fixed before confirmation data are collected. $A_{s,d}$ combines four cell means and $C_d$ combines eight, so independent errors of variance $\sigma^2$ per mean give $\mathrm{Var}(A_{s,d})=4\sigma^2$ and $\mathrm{Var}(C_d)=8\sigma^2$; $C_d$ therefore sets the sample size. The number of days $n$ for a 95\% interval half-width $h=0.5$ log$_2$ units satisfies $n\ge(t_{n-1,0.975}\,s_C/h)^2$, where $s_C$ is a conservative pilot estimate of the standard deviation of $C_d$. Six independent days is the minimum, and $n$ increases as the calculation requires. The precision target is fixed in advance, the calculation is retained, and data collection continues to the fixed sample size regardless of significance. If resources cannot meet the target, the extension is reported as exploratory. Pilot and confirmatory data are kept separate. The secondary family comprises the four MCOLN1 $\Delta_{a,s}$ contrasts and the primary-form $A_M$, $A_T$ and $C$ contrasts for LAMP1 and CTSD (ten tests, Holm adjustment). 3T and non-targeting-background comparisons are exploratory. The second-siRNA confirmation re-estimates the same primary contrast with its own complete blocks and interval; the two siRNAs are reported side by side and not pooled.

\textbf{Synthesis versus stability.} Mature RNA integrates production and degradation \cite{Rabani2011}. Before a difference is called a transcriptional defect, the prespecified WT/3A contrasts are repeated with a short, pilot-qualified 4-thiouridine pulse ending at 6 h, and newly synthesized MCOLN1 and a secondary target are quantified. Controls are matched unlabeled/background-purification samples, input RNA, labeled/unlabeled recovery standards, cell-count normalization, and checks for labeling toxicity and treatment-dependent incorporation (recovery standards control purification, and incorporation is compared across treatments to control cellular nucleoside uptake). Pulse duration and performance criteria are frozen before confirmation; if incorporation differs between treatments, the assay does not support differential synthesis. Concordant nascent and mature RNA supports a production interpretation, and discordance triggers RNA-stability/processing studies. Intronic RT-qPCR is an exploratory precursor-RNA assay. Without a qualified nascent-RNA assay, the endpoint is reported as target-gene RNA abundance.

\textbf{MiT/TFE compensation.} Overexpressed TFE3 induces MCOLN1 and other lysosomal genes, including in TFEB-depleted HeLa cells \cite{Martina2014}. TFE3 RNA, protein and localization are measured in the prespecified WT/3A comparison and the no-rescue controls. The knockdown background (TFEB-only or TFEB + TFE3) is chosen in the core pilot and frozen. When TFE3 activity or residual induction changes between genotypes, the key contrasts are repeated with TFEB-only, TFE3-only and combined depletion at matched total siRNA, two individual TFE3 reagents and WT-TFEB rescue, and viability and rescue expression are rechecked in each background. Persistence under competent combined depletion marks a TFEB/TFE3-independent route; loss under TFE3 depletion indicates family-context dependence. MITF and other routes remain candidates.

\subsection{Mechanistic Follow-Up with Defined Decision Rules}
\textbf{AMPK and Akt dependence.} WT/3A, both architectures and all three treatments are prespecified for R03; selecting only the largest R02 difference is exploratory. PRKAA1 and PRKAA2 are co-depleted with two independent siRNA pairs in separate experiments, alongside non-targeting, each single-target depletion, no-rescue and mock controls; total siRNA burden is matched, including the concurrent TFEB siRNA. Each catalytic subunit, MK-8722-induced p-ACC, p-S6K, p-AKT(S473), viability, nuclear exposure and rescue abundance are verified at the relevant times. HeLa cells lack LKB1 \cite{Fogarty2010}, so AMPK Thr172 phosphorylation relies on other upstream kinases such as CaMKK$\beta$; p-ACC and p-AMPK(T172) confirm MK-8722 engagement, and AMPK conclusions refer to this LKB1-deficient background. If Akt activity differs between the drugs, WT/3A is compared under a pharmacological Akt inhibitor to separate the Akt and AMPK routes at S467. A selective loss of the MK-8722 response with a retained Torin1 response supports stimulus-dependent AMPK involvement, and Torin1 serves as a comparator. If both responses fall, loss of cellular competence and altered baseline expression are evaluated. A near-endogenous, RNAi-resistant AMPK rescue with a matched empty-vector arm gives the target-specific causal test; the required isoform/complex and rescue constructs are sequence-defined at procurement. Without it, the finding is reported as convergent RNAi evidence for involvement. Kinase or phosphatase identity at the C-terminal sites comes from the phosphosite module below.

\textbf{Phosphosite specificity.} In WT tagged and untagged samples, C-terminal phosphorylation is measured together with total TFEB at baseline and after treatment. Phospho-antibodies are tested with phosphatase-treated material, unmodified/phosphorylated peptide controls and site-mutant controls, because adjacent residues can affect epitope recognition. Loss of signal in 3A establishes antibody specificity, and physiological dephosphorylation is shown by a phosphatase-sensitive, treatment-dependent change in WT. If specificity is unresolved, targeted LC--MS/MS with site-localization evidence, peptide standards and total-TFEB normalization is used, and ambiguous adjacent-site assignments are reported as such. Both architectures are enriched through a validated common TFEB epitope outside the altered region, with input/recovery documentation and IgG or bead controls, since GFP-only capture would exclude the untagged arm. Relative phosphopeptide abundance is reported with total-TFEB normalization; absolute site occupancy requires calibration. 3T is analyzed with an assay capable of detecting threonine phosphorylation. This follow-up depends on instrument access and assay development.

\textbf{Promoter engagement.} R05 starts after a qualified RNA-production difference and comparable nuclear exposure are established. TFEB occupancy near validated MCOLN1 and LAMP1 regulatory regions is tested by ChIP--qPCR at matched collection times, with input, matched IgG, TFEB-depleted/no-rescue material, a negative genomic region and a positive locus with demonstrable WT enrichment. A common TFEB antibody outside the changed region is used, with equivalent recovery, chromatin fragmentation and crosslinking conditions validated across architectures. Matched cell equivalents are used and percent input is reported, with a validated spike-in approach if global recovery differs, since percent input does not correct for nuclear TFEB abundance or antibody accessibility. Reduced occupancy supports altered engagement once these checks pass. Comparable occupancy with different nascent RNA motivates cofactor studies. If the checks fail, occupancy is reported as unresolved.

\begin{equation}
A^{X-Y}_{s,d}=(Y_{3A,X,s,d}-Y_{WT,X,s,d})-(Y_{3A,Y,s,d}-Y_{WT,Y,s,d}).
\end{equation}
$A^{E-L}$ is the EGFP component and $A^{L-U}$ the linker/stop-position component. \subsection{Additional Workload and Feasibility}
The extension retains six modules after M01--M12: R01, verify the five constructs and qualify expression/depletion; R02, run the factorial comparison, second-siRNA confirmation and conditional RNA-production/family-context checks; R03, test AMPK and Akt dependence; R04, resolve phosphosite specificity; R05, test promoter occupancy once its entry criteria are met; and R06, attribute an architecture effect to EGFP or the linker with the two linker-only constructs. Weeks 9--12 form a provisional preparation/pilot window. R04--R05 and the conditional R02/R03 additions depend on assay development, resources and preceding results. The eight-week core plan excludes this extension.

At the minimum six confirmatory days and two culture wells per condition, the 18 research conditions alone require 216 wells. Including controls, the primary block requires $45\times6\times2=540$ culture wells, and the second-siRNA WT/3A confirmation requires $39\times6\times2=468$: 1,008 wells \emph{per assay and endpoint} across the two campaigns. R06, when triggered, adds $51\times6\times2=612$ wells per assay and endpoint. These are lower-bound planning counts; they exclude the independent pilot, the six-point destructive imaging series, RNA/protein duplicates on separate plates, additional doses, clone checks, R03--R05 and technical PCR reactions. Scaling to multiwell plates is set by a density/delivery pilot. A physical plate map with all controls is released before execution. If capacity is insufficient, R01/R02 are completed first and the remaining modules are deferred, with all failed and null records retained.
