\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The proposed design links molecular identity to a testable localization phenotype. A correct DNA sequence is necessary but does not establish that the fusion preserves endogenous TFEB regulation. Fluorescent tagging and overexpression may alter abundance, interactions, and distribution. The matched EGFP-only vector controls tag-associated fluorescence and background; it does not prove that the fusion is functionally equivalent to endogenous TFEB. An absence of redistribution would therefore require checks of construct identity, intact protein expression, treatment response, and acquisition conditions before rejecting the biological hypothesis.

The localization assay measures redistribution rather than autophagic flux or lysosomal degradative activity. Nuclear accumulation can also accompany lysosomal dysfunction \cite{Settembre2012}; it cannot alone establish increased clearance. Moreover, an endpoint nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio does not separate increased import from reduced export, because TFEB export is itself regulated \cite{Napolitano2018}. Comparison with endogenous TFEB or additional functional assays would address these limitations, but they are outside this basic cloning-and-localization design.
