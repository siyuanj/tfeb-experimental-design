\section{Rationale and Research Basis}
TFEB is a member of the MiT/TFE transcription-factor family and a major transcriptional regulator of the autophagy--lysosome pathway. Under nutrient-rich conditions, mTORC1-dependent phosphorylation favors cytoplasmic retention of TFEB. During nutrient starvation or mTOR inhibition, TFEB accumulates in the nucleus and activates genes involved in lysosomal biogenesis and cellular clearance \cite{Settembre2012,RoczniakFerguson2012}. Nutrient-dependent control of nuclear export also contributes to TFEB distribution \cite{Napolitano2018}.

HeLa cells expressing TFEB--GFP have already been used to measure nuclear accumulation after starvation or Torin1 treatment \cite{RoczniakFerguson2012}. The present course project applies this established biological response to evaluate a newly designed reporter; it does not claim discovery of the mechanism. Its primary readout is the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic distribution of the fusion protein; additional controlled modules test reversibility, endogenous consistency and downstream function. Molecular identity must be established before that distribution can be interpreted.

\subsection{Research Objectives and Hypothesis}
The first objective is to construct and sequence-verify an EGFP-tagged TFEB mammalian expression plasmid. The second is to test nutrient- and drug-dependent redistribution and recovery using reporter and endogenous measurements. The third is to assess selected phospho-site contributions and TFEB-dependent transcriptional and turnover responses. The replication hypothesis is that starvation increases the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic fluorescence ratio relative to time-matched nutrient-replete culture, and that Torin1 increases the ratio relative to its matched vehicle control. A separate research objective tests whether C-terminal S466/S467/S469 substitution effects depend on the EGFP fusion and stimulus. That extension is motivated by unresolved interpretation of this cluster and uses its own factorial design and decision rules.
